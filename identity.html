Basic principles of scale and color

One arc.Three dials.No exceptions.

The Memory Orb is a lens, a hand controller and an editor. The identity borrows its geometry, its dials and its deadpan telemetry, then locks them so nobody can improve them.

01The core container

The optical arcis the grid.

Berkeley Rep’s ticket, cut in half, became the B and then every layout. The Vesper equivalent is the curve where the glass of the orb meets its barrel: the horizontal arc that divides the lit memory from the mechanism.

Every page, pack and screen is cut by that one curve. Above it: the image, the headline, the lit thing. Below it: telemetry, six cells, always the same six. Nothing crosses the arc except the red line that marks it.

The lens itself is the letterform. The O of the mark is the tall, narrow ellipse of the front glass, seen at an angle, with the red band beside it.

02The typographic engine

Vesper Ring.The dials set the type.

Vesper Ring is drawn as one variable family with three axes, one per physical ring. It never appears at an unset value: headlines are only ever extended (width 112–138) and always flush-left, and every line is scaled to fill its zone exactly. That is the MAM rule: the in-house team can type anything, and cannot make it look wrong.

The type is also telemetry. As the FIELD ring sweeps the forest away, the letterforms widen and pick up optical blur. VARIANT snaps the weight between three detents. TIME leans the whole line forward or back by how far the held layer has drifted from the host clock. The live site does exactly this. Turn the dials on it, or here:

Axis 110 · FLDWidth 112 → 138. Optical blur 0 → 1.2 px. Driven by the field ring.
Axis 112 · VARWeight in three detents: 560 · 710 · 860. Driven by the variant ring.
Axis 114 · TIMSlant −10° → +10°, proportional to the cake’s offset from host time. Driven by the time ring.
ModeWarm memory (amber, 3300 K) or isolation (cyan, 5600 K). Changes accent only.
SettingCaps, flush-left, fit to zone. Telemetry in a monospaced companion, never mixed with headline faces.
Memory

Prototype note: the live pages use Anybody (variable width and weight, Google Fonts) as the working stand-in for a drawn Vesper Ring. The mapping, limits and fit rules above are the specification the final face must meet; the slant is applied as a transform until the face has a true slant axis.

03The analog seed

The hand is mightierthan the screen.

The Orb’s world is synthetic memory, so the identity starts with things that cannot be synthesised: real birthday mess. Three raw sources, scanned flat at 1,200 dpi, then used as the only texture layer on the site and as texture maps in the 3D scenes.

  1. Frosting smear. Buttercream dragged across white card with a palette knife. Gives the directional streak.
  2. Candle wax on acetate. Dripped and pressed, backlit on a lightbox. Gives the amber blobs and drip edges.
  3. Scratched glass. A lens blank scored with a needle and graphite. Gives the long, uneven scratches across the dark blocks.

Hand marks on top: red marker underlines and rings, drawn on paper, scanned, never redrawn in a vector tool.

Stand-in, not scans. The three textures shown and used on this site were generated procedurally to the above descriptions, because no physical scans exist yet. They are placeholders for the real ones. Swap the three PNGs in assets/site/ and the whole site changes.

04The unbreakable spec

Their handsare cut off.

Void#0B0B0C · ground
Amber#F2A31B · warm memory, 3300 K
Steel#C5CED3 · hardware, telemetry
Cyan#4FC3D0 · isolation, 5600 K
Red band#C8301E · signal only
  1. Five colours, no others. Amber and cyan never share a block: a scene is warm or it is isolated. Red only marks the arc, the ring band and hand marks.
  2. One grid. Twelve columns, one gutter. Every section is the same pair of hemispheres: tele strip, headline zone, copy zone, evidence zone.
  3. Headlines are fit, not sized. Each line is scaled to its zone width. Short lines are huge, long lines are small; nobody chooses a size.
  4. Six cells, one bar. Solid/ghost · aperture · variant · light · kelvin · snapshot. Fixed to the bottom edge of every screen, same order, same widths.
  1. Copy is cut to fit. Headlines ≤ 3 lines of ≤ 28 characters. A longer title is rewritten, not shrunk.
  2. Texture comes from the seed folder only. No stock grain, no gradients, no glow.
  3. Motion has one job: it shows a dial moving. Nothing animates on its own.
  4. No sixth colour, no second headline face, no centred type. If a brief seems to need one, the brief is wrong.
