The orb reads a plate and makes the cake

The reference clips show cakes nobody built. The Memory Weaver now builds them. A recipe is not a stored model: it is the plate's description, compiled into the clicks of five rings and performed where you can watch the hand.

  1. Ring 1 to RECIPE. Ring 2 picks a shelf: the film's six, the 41 archetypes, the 77 clips.
  2. Ring 3 picks a plate. Its two icing colours and its inscription load into the orb.
  3. Ring 5 builds it. Touch any ring while it works and the book lets go; the cake stays as it is, yours to finish.

The second edit: every cake, multiplied

The first edit cuts between pictures. This one puts pictures inside each other: the film, its segmentation, its depth, the coded stage and the synthetic plates in one frame. Eight techniques, each a small function you can reuse.

  1. Slits: one film moment four ways in sliding bands (plate, segment, depth and edge, coded 3D).
  2. Cut-out: film cut-outs standing in the coded room while its camera circles.
  3. Swap: a cast held still, the cake under them changed every half beat.
  4. Parade: all 77 cakes cut from their plates, three rows moving against each other.
  5. Blend: a synthetic plate and the coded build of the same cake, by slats, wipe, strobe and lighten.
  6. Grid: the same breath 1, 4, 9, then 20 times, the light going on one beat.
  7. Rewind: those 20 backwards, the flames returning together.
  8. Stack: three breaths lightened into one frame; 42 builds under the film's mist.

One grid, one bar: the corpus normalized

Every plate is zoomed and shifted until its cake stands where the orb puts its own (centre, crown line, width), and timed so its event falls on beat 3 of one four-beat bar. The orb then builds each plate's recipe and is filmed on that same grid for that same bar, with the party of its own era cut from the plates standing behind. Any frame can follow any other and the cake does not move.
