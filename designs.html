STELLINE · THE DETAILS, RE-MADE

The details she works on in the film (the beetle's eyes, the writing on the cake, the candles and the light they put on the children) can be changed on the orb. In CUT, ring 2 lists them after the parts (CAKE WRITING, CAKE FLAMES, BEETLE RIGHT EYE, BOY FLAME), and ring 4 sets them. The change is made from the film's own pixels, frame by frame, so it plays with the film in WATCH and stands under the bench in BUILD. OTHERWISE picks a random version and ZERO puts the film back.

Tap an image to switch between the film's frame and the re-made one. Each caption ends with what the blind critic said the last time it saw that design.

How each is made

DetailMade from
Eye focusFor each eye on each frame: the frame's own eye with the fine detail of the nearest frame where that eye was in focus laid in (light and shape stay the frame's), or the frame softened around the eye. The two eyes are tracked separately.
WritingThe film's writing is taken off by carrying the cake top from before she wrote onto each frame (the top is a plane, so one homography carries it). New writing made of her own characters (生 日 快 乐 and the Z, lifted off frame 30) is re-set on the writing's plane in that frame's light. Typed words (安娜, JOE, ANA, 2049) are piped in a drawn hand instead.
FlamesEach flame is tracked; blown out, it is filled from the rows around it with its glow, and a wick is left. The light it gave goes with it, using the ratios measured when the film blows the candles out at 03:46: red to 0.41, green to 0.34, blue to 0.15 where the warm light fell. Cool light (the wall's ring) stays.

The blind critic, five rounds

Each round, a new critic saw real frames mixed with re-made ones (full frame plus an enlarged crop), was not told how many of each, and said which were altered. After round 2, real frames shown with a design were at least 8 frames away from it, so the critic could not just diff two copies of the same moment.

What passes and what does not, honestly: the film's own pixels re-arranged pass most often (both eyes sharp passed in four of five rounds; one sharp eye and one soft passed twice; writing re-set from her own characters passed every time in round 3 and half the time since). Light changes are the hardest: a critic who sees the same scene lit two ways can measure it. Typed words have never passed.
