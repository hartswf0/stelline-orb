A REAL MEMORY IS A MIST

Stelline tells K that a memory is not more detail but how it is recalled, and that "anything real should be a mist." This page cuts the scene from 1:49 into segments, marks what in each is made and what is real, and renders the made parts the way she describes them: as light without edges, with each earlier moment still hanging behind the present one.

THE RENDERS

How each is made: every tracked frame of the segment (12 a second) becomes a field of points, sampled where the picture is bright or has edges. A point's depth is the moment it was filmed. What the memory is made of (cake, table, children, candle, by the film's own mattes) emits; the real room (Stelline, K, the booth) is kept as faint dust; the children, dim in the frame and the point of the memory, are lifted. The present frame stands in front and older ones fade back over about a second. The renderer is AFTERLIGHT's spacetime splatter, used as it is.

THE WOODEN HORSE

In this clip the horse memory itself never appears: from 4:19 we see only Stelline at the viewer's eyepiece and K's face. There are no frames of the horse to segment here, so nothing on this page stands in for it. Given the furnace sequence from earlier in the film, it can be rendered the same way: the boy and the horse emitting, the furnace room as dust, and each moment of the chase hanging behind the next. Seen through the viewer it would be the one memory on this page that is not made, and the mist would be the test of that: a lived memory should hold together as light where a made one comes apart.

The weaver builds the made part; this page shows what it becomes when it is recalled. Mist (the tracked regions with their felt weight) is the same idea as an instrument.
